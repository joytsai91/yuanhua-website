Equipment overview

Built on a commitment to environmental protection, this system further refines FRIENDLY, the vacuum-distillation water-soluble waste fluid treatment system that handles a wide variety of waste fluids. By combining proprietary membrane separation technology with vacuum distillation, it treats emulsion-type oily waste fluid, previously difficult to handle, to a level where it can be discharged directly into the sewer.
FRIENDLY–Plus takes environmental protection further, giving you a more economical and safer solution.

  • Lower environmental impact
  • Lower costs
  • No foul odor
  • Less water use
  • Lower treatment energy
  • No open flame
  • Lighter workload

Drastically reduces waste fluid discharge

Until now, waste fluid generated at production plants has been handed straight to industrial waste disposal companies. By adopting FRIENDLY–Plus, you can sharply reduce waste fluid volume without major expense and cut waste fluid disposal costs substantially.

Emulsion-type oily waste fluid can be treated to a level where it can be discharged directly into the sewer.

Combining membrane separation and vacuum distillation

Proprietary membrane technology for efficient separation of oily waste fluid

Oily waste fluid is passed through a separation membrane bonded to the inside of porous fibers, which lets only water (including low-molecular-weight substances and ions) permeate and separates it from the oil (including high-molecular-weight substances and suspended matter). Highly turbulent flow inside the membrane maintains a high permeation rate without clogging the pores, ensuring stable treatment. The membrane is also kept clean by regular automatic cleaning with sponge balls or, when needed, by chemical cleaning. This membrane offers excellent heat and chemical resistance.

Emulsion separation unit

Safe treatment through efficient vacuum distillation

No open flame is used: vacuum distillation lowers the boiling point by reducing the pressure below atmospheric, so the waste fluid boils at a low temperature. Because the liquid stays cool, heat-induced chemical reactions and decomposition of its components are unlikely, making this a safe method that produces very little odor or harmful substances during treatment.

Vacuum distillation separation unit

1. Waste fluid is drawn in from the waste fluid drum.
2. The concentration vessel is depressurized.
3. The concentration vessel is heated and the fluid boils at a low temperature.
4. Antifoaming agent is added to prevent foaming during boiling.
5. The vapor produced flows to the refrigeration unit, where it condenses into reclaimed water.
6. The remaining concentrated waste fluid is concentrated several more times in the concentration vessel, then stored in the concentrate drum.
7. Reclaimed water is held temporarily in the cold water tank inside the unit, then transferred to the external reclaimed water drum.
8. During automatic cleaning, reclaimed water from outside the unit is drawn back into the concentration vessel.

Flow diagram

Flow diagram (left)

Flow diagram (right)

1. When waste fluid in the waste fluid intermediate tank reaches a set level, the transfer pump starts and sends it to the primary waste fluid tank.
2. On a supply signal from the emulsion separation unit, the primary waste fluid tank's transfer pump starts and sends a set amount of waste fluid to the feed circulation tank inside the unit.
3. Permeate separated in the emulsion separation unit collects in the wash tank; once it reaches a set level, it drains by gravity into the secondary waste fluid tank.
4. Concentrate separated in the emulsion separation unit drains by gravity into the concentrate drum. → "Disposed of as industrial waste"
5. Once the heating tank of the vacuum distillation unit reaches the set vacuum level, part of the permeate in the secondary waste fluid tank is drawn into the heating tank.
6. Reclaimed water separated in the vacuum distillation unit collects in the reclaimed water drum; once it reaches a set level, it drains by gravity out of the unit.
7. Concentrate separated in the vacuum distillation unit drains into the concentrate intermediate tank inside the unit; when that tank reaches a set level, the transfer pump starts and returns it to the primary waste fluid tank.
